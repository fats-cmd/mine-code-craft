# Prove the verb, then build the Tower

Great games, from Zelda to Opus Magnum, share one build method and one teaching method. They prove a single systemic verb in a crude prototype, put outsiders in front of it from the first week, and then teach that verb through the level itself: one idea at a time, a twist, then a combination. Failure is made cheap to retry, legible, and worth something. The learning-science and educational-game research points the same way. The best controlled study of educational games found children learned more, and chose to play about **7× longer**, when the maths *was* the game mechanic rather than a quiz gate in front of a reward. Games in general beat conventional teaching only modestly (**d≈0.29**) and are **not reliably more motivating**, so the motivation has to be designed in. Beginners learn programming best by tracing code before writing it, through rearrange-the-lines (Parsons) problems, and with a visible model of how code runs. Socially, players mostly stay for *small* groups and ambient company, not big guilds or global leaderboards, and leaderboards measurably hurt low-ranked players. For mine-codecraft this means five things. Make each Floor's Task *drive* the fight and the world. Order every concept as Predict, then Fix, then Code. Treat the Fall and Strikes as retrieval practice, not punishment. Build the proposed "Build" format on the nandgame ladder, where each gate a Climber builds becomes a part on later Floors. And make the Floor Hall a place of light, anonymous help and per-room histograms rather than global rank. Much of the developer evidence below reached us only through search-engine summaries, so treat quoted wording as provisional.

## Every great game started as one ugly, tested verb

Nintendo validates the system before the content. Breath of the Wild's physics-and-"chemistry" engine was first shown in a prototype dressed as the original top-down Zelda: trees cut for lumber, fields set alight. Nintendo of America's live coverage clarified that it was a 3D engine under a retro skin, so the prototype was deliberately ugly but ran the real systems **[PRIMARY, via coverage]** ([Zelda Universe](https://zeldauniverse.net/2017/03/09/zelda-team-discusses-breath-of-the-wilds-development-and-2d-zelda-prototype-at-gdc/); [Miketendo64](https://miketendo64.com/2017/03/01/nintendo-of-americas-twitter-coverage-of-the-gdc-breath-of-the-wild-panel/)). Director Hidemaro Fujibayashi described the shift from "additive" to **"multiplicative design"**, in which objects react to the player and to each other ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/99265/how-breath-of-the-wild-challenged-conventions-in-zelda-games)). Super Mario 3D Land's first prototype represented Mario as **a square block** **[PRIMARY, Iwata Asks via summary]** ([Iwata Asks](https://iwataasks.nintendo.com/interviews/3ds/super-mario-3d-land/0/3/)). Tears of the Kingdom's Ultrahand was pitched by building vehicles "using only the mechanisms and parts available in the previous game" **[PRIMARY]** ([Nintendo Ask the Developer](https://www.nintendo.com/sg/interview/totk/04.html)). An early field demo convinced the team that climbing was fun after Miyamoto spent about an hour just climbing trees ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/when-miyamoto-first-played-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-h-1793017234)).

The indie and puzzle games began even smaller. Outer Wilds started as Alex Beachum's USC thesis. Before it had a structure, he built an **"emotional prototype"**: roasting marshmallows by a campfire until the sun goes supernova ([The Examined Game](https://www.theexaminedgame.com/were-all-going-to-die-creating-outer-wilds-alex-beachum-creative-director/)). Portal grew from Narbacular Drop, a DigiPen student game. Kim Swift said playtesting was the biggest thing she learned at Valve: her student team tested only in its final month, while Valve **playtested from Portal's first week of production** **[PRIMARY]** ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/student-union/)). A near-weekly test, discuss and change cadence is reported but not traced to a primary quote **[SECONDARY]** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/analysis-of-game-concepts-and-player-learning-in-portal)). Valve writer Chet Faliszek summed up the attitude in a headline: **"Playtesters aren't idiots, it's you"** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/valve-s-faliszek-playtesters-aren-t-idiots-it-s-you)). Baba Is You began as a Nordic Game Jam 2017 entry. Arvi Teikari warned that "solo developers can easily become blind to a lot of the difficulty" and relied on beta testers **[PRIMARY]** ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-baba-is-you/)). Supergiant made Early Access **"the very first decision"** on Hades, to test the idea fast and keep growing it if players liked it **[PRIMARY]** ([RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/interview/20348-supergiant-games-interview-with-greg-kasavin-and-darren-korb-hades-ii-early-access-feedback-boss-themes-the-ending-coffee-and-the-future)). Factorio's public weekly Friday Facts devlog shows the same openness over years ([Seven Years of Friday Facts](https://spieswl.github.io/blog/2020/seven-years-of-factorio-friday-facts)).

Two engineering lessons stand out for mine-codecraft. First, Baba Is You never hardcodes rule combinations: word blocks are parsed into rules at runtime, because there are too many combinations to enumerate **[PRIMARY]** ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026590/Reading-the-Rules-of-Baba)). A Build Floor of switches and gates needs the same thing: one small, general evaluator with content composed from its parts. Second, TotK's "new feature from old parts" rule gives a cheap discipline for each new Floor: express its mechanic with the existing Formats and props before writing new systems. mine-codecraft's equivalent of BotW's chemistry engine is the Task itself, a real Python or JS challenge graded by a real runner. **The game should prove a Task is fun as a fight in a greybox, with someone who didn't write it playing, before building a Floor's Setting.** The gaps are real, though. No source covered FromSoftware's prototyping process, and the full GDC talks (BotW 2017, Portal 2008, Outer Wilds 2020, Celeste 2017) could not be read.

## The level is the tutorial, and code should be read before it is written

Nintendo teaches through staged, safe exposure. Miyamoto designed World 1-1 so players "gradually and naturally understand what they're doing," and he watched testers play without explaining anything **[PRIMARY, via coverage]** ([Nintendo Insider](https://www.nintendo-insider.com/shigeru-miyamoto-shares-the-design-process-behind-world-1-1-in-super-mario-bros/)). The first Goomba is placed so a death teaches the jump, and the Super Mushroom moves *toward* the player so they learn it is good ([GoNintendo](https://www.gonintendo.com/s/100479-miyamoto-explains-how-the-player-is-taught-that-goombas-are-bad-and-mushrooms-are-good)). Koichi Hayashida builds Mario stages on **kishōtenketsu**: introduce an idea safely, develop it, twist it, then conclude with a showcase. 3D Land kept strictly to one concept per level, while 3D World used the twist to bring in a second mechanic **[PRIMARY, via summary; paraphrased]** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-secret-to-i-mario-i-level-design)). BotW's Great Plateau is a walled sandbox where every system can be tried before the world opens **[SECONDARY]** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plateau)).

Portal turns this into a whole game. Faliszek said **"80 percent of Portal 1 was a training arc"**. For Portal 2, Valve swapped bouncing energy balls for lasers because lasers always show how they will affect the environment **[PRIMARY, talk report]** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-i-portal-2-i-making-a-sequel-to-a-perfect-game)). The first portal is placed so players see themselves through it, because testers understood portals "much more quickly" that way **[PRIMARY]** ([Portal developer commentary](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary)). The Witness teaches its rule language with no words at all, and many panels unlock nothing; they exist only to teach a later idea **[SECONDARY]** ([Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/the_witness/b/playstation4/archive/2013/06/14/five-features-that-define-the-game.aspx)). Human Resource Machine starts with **two commands and adds more, up to eleven**. A non-programmer reviewer said watching the office worker walk through every step "makes it much easier to understand" ([Boing Boing](https://boingboing.net/2015/10/19/human-resource-machine-game.html/amp); [Macworld](https://www.macworld.com/article/228439/human-resource-machine-can-teach-you-coding-whether-you-realize-it-or-not.html)). One popular phrase, "train, test, twist," could not be traced to Valve or any primary source and should not be cited.

The cautionary tales are just as specific. SpaceChem's own postmortem says "our tutorial is awful, unusable; the game was really, really hard; and it's really, really long," and **only 2% of players finished** its 40+ hour curve ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-zachtronics-industries-i-spacechem-i-)). Reviewers say HRM later "made leaps too large for beginners" ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-human-resource-machine/)). Zachtronics' later games replaced tutorials with dense in-fiction manuals: TIS-100's was 14 pages, printed, marked up and scanned back in ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-things-we-create-tell-people-who-we-are-designing-zachtronics-i-tis-100-i-)). That suits a self-selected audience, not newcomers. Turing Complete players likewise say you must "RtFM" and lean on the Discord ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1444480/discussions/0/5143704785434709480/)). Factorio's developers wrote that "if the first 15 minutes of a game feels shitty, there is big chance that the player will not play any further," and cut a tutorial of about 22 message dialogs **[PRIMARY]** ([FFF #261](https://factorio.com/blog/post/fff-261)).

CS-education research supplies the order of steps for a coding game. Novices should be competent at **tracing code before writing it**, per a Raspberry Pi Foundation synthesis citing Lister et al. 2004 **[MODERATE, synthesis]** ([Raspberry Pi](https://static.raspberrypi.org/files/curriculum/quickreads/14-Pedagogy_Summary_Code_Tracing_V4_2023.pdf)). Parsons problems, where learners rearrange given lines, were solved **significantly faster than writing or fixing equivalent code with the same learning gains** in Ericson's work **[MODERATE–STRONG]** ([ITiCSE 2023 working group](https://strathprints.strath.ac.uk/86100/1/Ericson_etal_ITiCSE_2023_Conducting_multi_institutional_studies_of_Parsons_problems.pdf)). Worked examples help true novices most, but that benefit **disappears as expertise grows**, so worked steps should be faded out **[STRONG]** ([Wikipedia: Worked-example effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worked-example_effect)). Making the "notional machine" (a model of how code executes) explicit is an emerging recommendation, because novices' mental models are often wrong **[Theory + emerging evidence]** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16594v1)). In Weintrop and Wilensky's quasi-experiment, blocks produced larger early gains and more interest, but students saw them as "less authentic," and after moving to Java the groups did not differ **[MODERATE]** ([TOCE paper](https://ccl.northwestern.edu/2017/a3_weintrop_wilensky.pdf); [C&E 2019](https://www.terpconnect.umd.edu/~weintrop/papers/Weintrop&Wilensky_2019_C&E.pdf)).

Put together, this gives mine-codecraft a per-Floor template that comes from both Nintendo and the classroom. ***Ki*** is an ungraded Predict that traces one concept. ***Shō*** is a Fix, or a Parsons-style reorder, on the same concept. ***Ten*** is the twist: an off-by-one, recursion instead of a loop, or the other Language. ***Ketsu*** is the Examiner, a Code Task that combines them. The Testing Hall should be a Great Plateau: every Format tried safely before anything costs Integrity. The first 15 minutes should end with a real "I made this run" moment, not dialog boxes. And because a 3D world can show state, the notional machine can be made literal: variables as crates, the call stack as stacked platforms, a loop as a conveyor. Then tracing a Predict Task is watching the world, which is Portal's laser lesson applied to code.

## Failure should cost something, teach something, and leave something behind

FromSoftware and Supergiant reach opposite-looking answers from the same premise. Hidetaka Miyazaki asks, "If death is to be more than a mark of failure, how do I give it meaning?" He calls difficulty the studio's identity and draws the line at "impossible challenges" **[PRIMARY, New Yorker via coverage]** ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/elden-ring-creator-miyazaki-feels-apologetic-towards-players-defeated-by-his-games/)). He has described the death system as trial and error where "the difficulty is high, but always achievable" **[PRIMARY, IGN via a blog quote: SECONDARY transmission]** ([Reviewing the Cults](https://reviewingthecults.wordpress.com/2015/03/27/cult-video-game-director-hidetaka-miyazaki/)), and he balances for "fairness over difficulty" **[SECONDARY, forum repost]** ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/168566-dark-souls-iii/73647265)). Bonfires give a fixed restart point and a place to rest in a harsh world **[SECONDARY]** ([Wikipedia: Bonfire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonfire_(Dark_Souls))). Hades went the other way: its explicit early goal was to "reduce the sting of failure." Each death adds narrative and permanent upgrades, so players feel "the time you spent wasn't a waste" **[PRIMARY, via coverage]** ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-supergiant-weaves-narrative-rewards-into-i-hades-i-cycle-of-perpetual-death); [Inlander](https://www.inlander.com/culture/hades-writer-greg-kasavin-on-how-he-made-video-game-deaths-drive-a-feel-good-story-22725237)). Its dialogue system checks run state, such as a character met while Zagreus' health is low, and plays fitting pre-written lines. Kasavin calls it "the opposite of scripted" ([GeekDad](https://geekdad.com/2019/10/narrative-and-early-access-supergiants-greg-kasavin-discusses-hades-development/)). Outer Wilds keeps only knowledge across its time loop: progress is "all knowledge-gated," and players keep going "to answer their questions" **[PRIMARY, in fan-run interviews; wording may be paraphrased]** ([Medium](https://medium.com/@claire.boeck.172/interview-with-alex-beachum-ba7e21ee5d17)).

Celeste shows how to make the *remaining* failures fair. Maddy Thorson's write-up lists hidden forgiveness: coyote time, jump buffering, halved gravity at the jump peak, corner correction. These strip out deaths caused by input timing, so the deaths left read as the player's own error **[PRIMARY]** ([Celeste & Forgiveness](https://maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/)). Its difficulty is intended, but strawberries, B-sides and Assist Mode "are all there to help players find the challenge level that's right for them". Assist Mode offers separate toggles for speed, stamina, dashes and even invincibility, framed as a choice and not an easy mode **[PRIMARY quote]** ([VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/celeste-difficulty-assist-mode/)). A later patch softened its wording from "essential" to "intended" ([VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/celeste-assist-mode-change-and-accessibility/)). Jonathan Blow adds the content rule: the aha must reveal something true, not trick the player. "I'm not tricking you into feeling smart" **[PRIMARY]** ([PlayStation LifeStyle](https://www.playstationlifestyle.net/2016/01/04/jonathan-blow-the-witness-puzzles-im-not-tricking-feeling-smart)). Teikari spent "a very large part of the testing process" removing alternative solutions that taught nothing **[PRIMARY]** ([MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-baba-is-you/)). Hades' Heat difficulty system was largely ignored in Early Access until it was *rewarded* **[SECONDARY]** ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/hades-changes-early-access/)).

The research supports failure, but with conditions. Sinha and Kapur's meta-analysis of **53 studies (166 comparisons)** found that problem solving *before* instruction beat instruction-first, and that the effect grew with fidelity to productive-failure design. In that design, struggle is followed by explanation **[STRONG]** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.3102/00346543211019105)). The headline "twice a year of good teaching" comes from press releases, and critiques from the cognitive-load camp were not retrieved. Bjork's "desirable difficulties" research shows that spacing, interleaving and retrieval practice improve long-term retention even though learners *judge* them as worse: "What we can observe is performance, but what we have to infer is learning" **[STRONG, via secondary sources]** ([APS Observer](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/observer/desirable-difficulties)). **No study was found that tests a "lose a level on failure" mechanic for learning.**

For mine-codecraft's Integrity, Strikes and the Fall, this sets clear requirements. The inner loop of submit, see the failing case, edit and resubmit should be fast and free, like a Celeste respawn. The Integrity cost belongs to the attempt, not the keystroke. Every Counter should show the input, expected and actual output, and the concept being tested, because Portal's lasers beat bouncing balls. Grading should forgive noise such as whitespace, trailing newlines and equivalent output formats, never the concept. Fix Tasks must block degenerate "solutions" like hardcoding output, as Teikari pruned Baba's. A failed Examiner should be followed by a debrief, because without the instruction half, productive failure is just failure. It should also leave something permanent behind: a Pioneer Codex line, an Objection Journal page, or knowledge of the next phase. The Fall is defensible as a desirable difficulty **only if it lands the Climber on retrieval practice of prerequisite concepts.** Hades-style conditional lines ("fell a floor," "fifth attempt," "solved it in JS this time") turn the repetition into story. An Assist panel of separate toggles (more time, one revealed test case, a worked example), with unassisted clears keeping the prestige, follows Celeste. Any opt-in hard mode, such as banned built-ins or a step cap, must pay out, or players will ignore it.

## Learning has to be the mechanic, not a gate in front of it

The strongest single argument for mine-codecraft's design comes from Habgood and Ainsworth. In *Zombie Division*, the version where maths *was* the combat taught more in fixed time than a version where maths was a gate before the fun. Given free choice, children played it **about 7× longer** **[MODERATE]** ([Nottingham repository](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/23577510/motivating-children-to-learn-effectively-exploring-the-value-of-intrinsic-integration-in-educational-games)). This builds on Malone's distinction between *extrinsic* fantasy, which barely touches the skill, and *intrinsic* fantasy "intimately related to the use of the skill" **[Theory]** ([UCSB](https://cdgr.ucsb.edu/node/768)). The broader picture is sobering. Wouters et al.'s meta-analysis found serious games beat conventional instruction for learning (**d=0.29**) and retention (**d=0.36**), but were **not significantly more motivating**. They worked better with supplementary instruction, multiple sessions and group play **[STRONG]** ([Utrecht](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/a-meta-analysis-of-the-cognitive-and-motivational-effects-of-seri/)). DragonBox is the transfer warning. Players solved more problems and enjoyed it more, but did worse on a standard algebra post-test than students using a tutor, because the game "provides too much scaffolding" and never asks players to write equations **[MODERATE]** ([CMU](https://www.cmu.edu/homepage/computing/2014/spring/fun-and-games.shtml)). A later large RCT of DragonBox 12+ was more positive **[MODERATE, conflicting; found via a mirror, verify on SAGE]** ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/23328584231165919)). Hanus and Fox found badges and leaderboards in a course **lowered intrinsic motivation and exam scores** **[MODERATE]** ([Hanus profile](https://osu.academia.edu/MichaelHanus)).

The programming games show what intrinsic integration looks like in practice. Zachtronics puzzles accept **any working solution**, and then optimisation becomes optional style ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/perfectly-solving-opus-magnums-puzzles-is-impossible-but-thats-ok/)). The fiction is the frame, not the decoration. Shenzhen I/O came from Zach Barth reading bunnie Huang's blogs about working in Shenzhen ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_I/O)). HRM's inbox, outbox and floor tiles are input, output and memory ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Resource_Machine)). "Educational" framing hurt Zachtronics, though. Barth felt SpaceChem's theme made it "nearly impossible to discover, try, and buy." A curriculum project with Amplify frustrated the studio, so he made the library free for schools separately ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/postmortem-zachtronics-industries-i-spacechem-i-); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/why-last-call-bbs-is-the-last-call-for-indie-studio-zachtronics/)). Factorio, framed as a world rather than a lesson, passed **3.5 million copies by Christmas 2022** without discounts ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/factorio-has-sold-35-million-copies/)). Minecraft redstone players build entire CPUs, such as CHUNGUS2, which took 7 months and runs at 1 Hz, without being asked ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/a-1hz-cpu-made-in-minecraft)). That is the strongest evidence that honest, composable primitives produce the hand-crafting feeling the creator wants back.

That makes the proposed **Build** format the most promising addition, and nandgame, Nand2Tetris and Turing Complete supply its blueprint. In nandgame, "you are tasked with building a component that behaves according to a specification. This component can then be used as a building block in the next level." The ladder runs from Invert and AND through adders, latches, memory, the ALU and a computer, then on to assembly. It needs no maths beyond addition and subtraction ([Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/games/nandgame/); [GitHub solutions](https://github.com/simsieg/nandgame-solutions)). Nand2Tetris builds a 16-bit machine up to an OS from Nand alone and stresses separating abstraction from implementation ([Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/910789.The_Elements_Of_Computing_Systems)). Turing Complete players praise inventing their own instruction set. They also criticise that adding opcodes to a later CPU can break earlier ones while the level still passes, because the game "doesn't regression test" **[SECONDARY]** ([Hacker News](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=38925307)). A reviewer accepts its simplified timing as worth it for the difficulty curve ([Steam Community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1444480)). Mapped onto the Tower, Boole's Floor builds gates from a switch or NAND primitive. Later Floors hand back *the Climber's own* gates as sealed parts with pin specs, re-run their tests whenever they are reused, and offer a stock fallback so one bug never blocks the climb. The simulator should be idealised: one global tick, live wire colouring, step and play controls, and a truth-table checker. Physical realism costs more than it teaches. The DragonBox result argues that Build complements the real-syntax Formats and must not replace them: Code and Fix in standard Python and JS stay the transfer test.

Applied to bosses, intrinsic integration means the Climber's code or prediction should *govern* what happens: the punched-card program drives the Engine, and the Predict answer decides where the Examiner's next blow lands. A multiple-choice Quiz that merely unlocks a sword swing is the pattern Habgood and Ainsworth found inferior. The history works the same way. Miyazaki puts crucial plot "in the description of an item in your inventory" to spark imagination **[PRIMARY, via search summary]** ([Wikipedia: Hidetaka Miyazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidetaka_Miyazaki)). Barth argues "things we create tell people who we are" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-things-we-create-tell-people-who-we-are-designing-zachtronics-i-tis-100-i-)). A relay, a vacuum tube or a Lovelace note can carry two or three lines of real history that readers absorb and skimmers can skip.

## Small groups and anonymous help keep players; global ladders drive beginners away

The social evidence favours light, small-scale contact. Ducheneaut and Yee's study of more than 300,000 World of Warcraft characters found most players experience **"collective solitude"**: they play surrounded by others but not with them, play regularly with only a fraction of their guild, and do best in small, work-team-like groups **[MODERATE, large dataset]** ([SRI](https://www.sri.com/publication/fcd-publications/collective-solitude-and-social-networks-in-world-of-warcraft/)). In Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski's online-community study, **autonomy, competence and relatedness each independently predicted enjoyment and future play** **[MODERATE–STRONG]** ([ACU](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8q128/the-motivational-pull-of-video-games-a-self-determination-theory-approach)). Content alone does not hold learners. Harvard and MIT's edX data across 565 course runs and about 5.6M learners shows completion **fell** over six years, to around 3% (the snippet was truncated) **[STRONG, large dataset]** ([MIT TSL](https://tsl.mit.edu/research/the-mooc-pivot/)). In Scratch, users who remix more show larger programming repertoires, an associative rather than causal link **[MODERATE, large observational]** ([Microsoft Research](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/remixing.pdf)).

FromSoftware's asynchronous systems are the design pattern that fits. Miyazaki traces them to strangers who pushed his stalled car up a snowy hill and vanished before he could thank them **[PRIMARY, via coverage]** ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/elden-ring-and-dark-souls-summoning-system-was-inspired-by-miyazakis-car-breaking-down/)). He has spoken of "sharing of experiences," with players helping each other through hard parts ([Gematsu](https://www.gematsu.com/2011/02/dark-souls-miyazaki-on-dlc-multiplayer-demo-and-more)). He also opposes separate difficulty modes that would "segment and fragment the user base" **[SECONDARY]** ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/814380/discussions/0/1815422173027344395/?l=schinese)). On competition, Opus Magnum shows each solution's cost, cycles and area as **histograms against every other player**, with friends marked. A critic notes that solutions topping all three "basically don't exist beyond trivial problems," so nearly everyone has "something to feel good about" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/perfectly-solving-opus-magnums-puzzles-is-impossible-but-thats-ok/); [Punished Backlog](https://punishedbacklog.com/opus-magnum-the-gold-standard-of-puzzle-games/)). Ranked leaderboards, by contrast, help those near the top while **low-rank positions harm motivation** **[MODERATE]** ([Emerald review](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/intr-12-2021-0897/full/html)). Duolingo reports that a streak wager lifted **Day-7 retention by 14%**. It also capped Friend Streak at five invites, warning that scaling a social feature is not always desirable **[WEAK–MODERATE, company self-report]** ([Duolingo engineering](https://making.duolingo.com/how-streaks-keep-duolingo-learners-committed-to-their-language-goals); [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/product-lessons-friend-streak/)). Its leagues' reported 17% gain in learning time is second-hand **[WEAK]** ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/4/14/duolingo-how-the-15b-app-uses-gaming-principles-to-supercharge-dau-growth)). On toxicity, Riot found that penalties with explanations and chat-log evidence raised reform from about 50% to 65–70%. Feedback within minutes helped, priming messages had only small, short-lived effects, and Riot estimated **about 1% of players are persistently toxic** **[WEAK–MODERATE, company research via press]** ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2015/09/14/166255/a-video-game-algorithm-to-solve-online-abuse/)). Screeps adds two warnings: buying more CPU feels pay-to-win ([GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/reviews/screeps-the-javascript-programmers-mmo/)), and showing other players' code output raised a contested security dispute ([outsidetheasylum.blog](https://outsidetheasylum.blog/screeps/)).

For the Floor Hall, ambient co-presence is valuable on its own, but retention ties form in small teams of two to four. Tower of God's own team-based tests support this, though that evidence is fan-wiki only **[WEAK]** ([Tower of God wiki mirror](https://breezewiki.discard.no/towerofgod/wiki/Floors)). The Souls toolkit translates directly. Messages become constrained-vocabulary hint notes at a Task ("Try: base case"), bloodstains become "37% of Climbers failed test 4," ghosts become silhouettes of other Climbers at a checkpoint, and summon signs become opt-in rubber-duck help that rewards the helper. Remixing suggests unlocking other Climbers' accepted solutions *after* a pass, with any rendered code sandboxed and sanitised. Duels should be skill-matched, metrics should be shown as per-Floor or per-cohort histograms on trade-off axes (steps against length, gate count against delay), and performance must never be for sale. Because a Fall in a shared Hall is a public loss of status, it threatens both competence and relatedness. It should be quiet, temporary and protected for new Climbers. Rewards should signal mastery rather than gate progress, given Hanus and Fox.

## Conclusion

The studies and the studios agree more than either expects. The things a coding game is tempted to bolt on, such as badges, global ranks, dialog-box tutorials and quiz gates, are exactly what the evidence says fails. The things great games do for their own sake, such as legible feedback, failure that pays out, a world that teaches, and strangers who help and vanish, are what the learning science says works. The open risk for mine-codecraft is its most distinctive mechanic. No source tests whether losing a level on failure helps learning, and Celeste's and Hades' success with harsh content depended on making setbacks local, informative and rewarding. The Fall should therefore be treated as a hypothesis to instrument per room (drop-off after a Fall, retry rates, time to re-climb), not as settled design. SpaceChem's postmortem shows what happens when metrics only confirm that "it is hard."

The second insight concerns the creator's goal. The evidence that people still want to craft by hand comes not from educational games but from Minecraft redstone CPUs, Turing Complete's home-made instruction sets and TotK players out-building Nintendo. All three rest on honest, composable primitives and a sense of authorship. That argues for making the Build format and Climber-owned components the spine of the Tower's later Floors, from Boole's gates to Turing's machine. Then each Floor reuses what the Climber made below it, and reaching the top means having built, piece by piece, a computer they understand. Before committing, the GDC talks and interviews quoted here via summaries (BotW 2017, Hades 2021, Outer Wilds 2020, Barth's 2019 open-ended puzzle talk and the Portal commentary) deserve a full read.
