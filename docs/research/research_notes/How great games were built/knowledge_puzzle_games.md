## 1. How did each team prototype and iterate?

### Takeaway
Every one of these games started as a small, cheap prototype: a student thesis, a student project, a game jam entry, or a tech demo with an "emotional prototype". Each became great through constant playtesting, with outsiders playing from the very first week of production. For Portal the cycle was weekly. The teams treated tester confusion as the designer's fault, not the player's.

### Cited Findings
**Outer Wilds (Mobius Digital, Alex Beachum)**
- The game began in Fall 2012 as Beachum's USC Master's thesis and also as a USC Advanced Game Project for the 2012/2013 school year. — [The Examined Game, "We're All Going to Die – Creating Outer Wilds"](https://www.theexaminedgame.com/were-all-going-to-die-creating-outer-wilds-alex-beachum-creative-director/) **[secondary, with Beachum interview material]**; see also [Road to the IGF: Alex Beachum's Outer Wilds](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/road-to-the-igf-alex-beachum-s-i-outer-wilds-i-) **[primary interview]**
- Beachum had built several planetary tech demos but lacked a unifying idea. He then made an "emotional prototype" to set the mood: the player roasts marshmallows by a campfire until the nearby sun goes supernova. — [The Examined Game](https://www.theexaminedgame.com/were-all-going-to-die-creating-outer-wilds-alex-beachum-creative-director/); podcast [The Fourth Curtain: "Outer Wilds' Alex Beachum's Cool Weird Emotional Prototypes"](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/outer-wilds-alex-beachums-cool-weird-emotional-prototypes/id1671350556?i=1000702968300) **[primary, audio]**
- GDC 2020 talk "Curiosity-Driven Exploration: The Design of Outer Wilds" (Beachum and Loan Verneau). It traced the meta design "from early 2D prototypes to the final structure of the game's overarching mystery", and covered ways to inspire and reward curiosity and to communicate an unusual game structure. — [GDC news](https://www.gdconf.com/news/attend-gdc-and-learn-how-outer-wilds-nailed-curiosity-driven-game-design) **[primary session abstract]**
- A companion GDC 2020 talk, "The 4D Level Design of Outer Wilds", covered levels that "control the pacing and delivery of narrative information within a large-scale physics simulation that changes the world over time". — [GDC news](https://gdconf.com/news/see-4d-level-design-outer-wilds-deconstructed-gdc-2020) **[primary abstract]**
- A Mobius dev update said playtests aimed to see how players reacted to clues. Some clues "were not registering with players as obviously as hoped" and needed polish through level design and narrative changes. Surface interactions were being smoothed so testers would get lost less easily. — [Mobius Digital, "Ship Damage and More"](https://www.mobiusdigitalgames.com/news/ship-damage-and-more) **[primary; post date unclear]**

**Portal (Valve; from DigiPen's Narbacular Drop)**
- Valve staff saw Narbacular Drop, a DigiPen student game, at a DigiPen job fair. The team then demoed it to Gabe Newell, got an offer on the spot to build it in full on Source, and started Portal in July 2005. — [MCV/Develop, "Student Union"](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/student-union/) **[primary interview]**
- Kim Swift said playtesting was the biggest thing she learned at Valve. Her student team had playtested Narbacular Drop only in its last month, too late to fix what they found. At Valve, playtesting began in Portal's very first week of production, and she was surprised by how rigorous it was. — [MCV/Develop, "Student Union"](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/student-union/); [Game Developer, "Thinking With Portals: Creating Valve's New IP"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/thinking-with-portals-creating-valve-s-new-ip) **[primary]**
- The playtest cadence is reported as almost weekly: test on Friday, discuss results on Monday, apply changes before the next Friday. — reported in search extracts around [Game Developer, "Analysis of Game Concepts and Player Learning in Portal"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/analysis-of-game-concepts-and-player-learning-in-portal) **[secondary; not verified to a primary quote]**
- Portal's GDC postmortem described a core team of about 10 people and an iterative process of merging story and gameplay. — [Game Developer, "Best of GDC: The Secrets of Portal's Huge Success"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/best-of-gdc-the-secrets-of-i-portal-i-s-huge-success) **[primary talk write-up]**
- At GDC 2008, the Portal developers said playtesters wandered around confused during a chase sequence instead of going where the designers wanted. The sequence was dropped. — [Destructoid, "GDC 08: Portal devs talk narrative, development, rat men"](https://www.destructoid.com/gdc-08-portal-devs-talk-narrative-development-rat-men/) **[secondary report of primary talk]**
- Chet Faliszek (Valve) argued for frequent external playtesting, under the headline "Playtesters aren't idiots, it's you". — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/valve-s-faliszek-playtesters-aren-t-idiots-it-s-you) **[primary]**
- Portal 2: each test chamber starts from one specific goal, and "playtesting is the most important tool" in building puzzles. A recurring failure in tests was players not noticing the elements they needed. — [Game Informer, "Thinking With Portals: Making a Portal 2 Test Chamber"](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2010/03/17/thinking-with-portals-making-a-test-chamber.aspx) **[primary designer interviews]**

**Baba Is You (Arvi "Hempuli" Teikari)**
- The game started as a Nordic Game Jam 2017 entry. The jam theme led Teikari to the idea of inverting what a concept means. — [MCV/Develop, "When We Made Baba Is You"](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-baba-is-you/) **[primary]**; [Road to the IGF: Hempuli Oy's Baba Is You](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-hempuli-oy-s-i-baba-is-you-i-) **[primary]**
- After the jam, Teikari had to choose between a sandbox toy and a challenging puzzle game. He chose the puzzle game. He also noted that "solo developers can easily become blind to a lot of the difficulty", so beta testers tuned difficulty. — [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-baba-is-you/) and related interviews ([Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/ng-en/baba-is-you-interview)) **[primary]**
- From the start he wanted a "very dynamic" rule system. Hardcoding every combination was impossible because there are too many words, so rules are parsed at runtime from the word blocks. His GDC 2020 talk "Reading the Rules of Baba Is You" covers turning physical tiles into sentences the game logic understands, the data structure behind it, and its many iterations. — [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026590/Reading-the-Rules-of-Baba); [slides PDF](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2020/presentations/Reading%20the%20rules_Teikari_Arvi.pdf); [Game Developer video post](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-understanding-the-rules-of-i-baba-is-you-i-) **[primary]**

**Celeste (Maddy Thorson, Noel Berry, et al.)**
- Thorson's GDC 2017 level design talk, as reported, said playtests showed players acting in unexpected ways and often skipping large parts of levels. Celeste needed more than 300 platforming screens, and movement options were added so a screen could be cleared in different ways. — [VidaExtra (Spanish)](https://vidaextra.com/aventura-plataformas/creadores-celeste-pusieron-pinchos-mortales-a-mala-leche-niveles-para-enganarte-forma-brillante-posible) **[secondary report of primary talk]**

### Inferences
- Portal's weekly cycle of test, discuss and change is the most transferable process lesson. For mine-codecraft, every Floor and challenge set could get an outside playtester (someone who did not write it) from its first greybox, not at the end.
- Beachum's "emotional prototype" maps directly onto a Floor: before building the challenges, prototype the 2-minute moment a Floor is about (for example, Babbage's engine refusing to run) to fix its tone.
- The architecture of Baba Is You (rules parsed at runtime from world objects, never hardcoded combinations) is a model for any "circuit building" or composable-rule Floor. Build a small, general evaluator and let content combine its pieces.

### Gaps
- Could not read full transcripts of the GDC talks (Outer Wilds 2020, Portal 2008, Celeste 2017). The Vault and the press pages were blocked for direct fetch.
- No primary source found for Celeste's origin as a short jam prototype (often said to be a PICO-8 game made in about four days). Unverified here.
- The Narbacular Drop to Portal timeline details beyond the July 2005 start were not checked against a second source.

## 2. How do they teach without text tutorials, introduce one idea at a time, then combine ideas?

### Takeaway
All five games treat the level itself as the tutorial. Portal is in effect one long "training arc" that introduces a single tool per chamber and then layers tools together. The Witness and Baba Is You teach a rule language only through puzzles built to isolate one rule. Outer Wilds gates progress on knowledge alone. The specific phrase "train, test, twist" could not be traced to Valve or any primary source.

### Cited Findings
- Portal commentary: the first portal is placed so players see themselves through it, because early playtesters understood portals "much more quickly" that way. The team calls it critical that players grasp portals quickly. — [Portal developer commentary (Portal Wiki)](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary) **[primary, in-game commentary transcribed]**
- Portal commentary describes the whole game as training: much of it introduces tools and then layers them into harder puzzles. Earlier builds let players stumble through the opening without understanding, and that undercut teaching later concepts. — [Portal developer commentary](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary) **[primary]**
- At GDC 2012, Chet Faliszek said "80 percent of Portal 1 was a training arc". Portal 2 had to teach new players while still interesting returning ones. Valve swapped Portal 1's bouncing energy balls for laser beams because lasers stay put and always show the player how they will affect the environment, which makes the mechanic more legible. — [Game Developer, "GDC 2012: Portal 2: Making a sequel to a 'perfect' game"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-i-portal-2-i-making-a-sequel-to-a-perfect-game) **[primary talk report]**
- An analysis of how Portal 2's mechanics add depth through combination. — [Game Developer, "Mechanics, Depth, and Portal 2"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/mechanics-depth-and-portal-2) **[secondary]**. Mark Brown on Valve's methods: [GMTK, "Valve's Secret Weapon"](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/valves-secret-weapon) **[secondary]**
- The Witness: Blow set out to make a game built on non-verbal communication. Puzzle rules are never explained in words, and the puzzles themselves teach the rules. — [Wikipedia, The Witness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Witness_(2016_video_game)) **[secondary summary of developer intent]**
- Many Witness panels unlock nothing in the world. They exist to teach a concept that later puzzles use. — [Game Informer preview, 2013](https://gameinformer.com/games/the_witness/b/playstation4/archive/2013/06/14/five-features-that-define-the-game.aspx) **[secondary, based on Blow demo]**
- Blow criticized the convention of "a little character telling you every obvious thing over and over again for hours… that is not the way that I like to do it". — [Edge "Post Script" interview via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20160210/282170765186464) **[primary]**
- Baba Is You: rules are pushable word blocks (for example BABA IS YOU, WALL IS STOP). Rearranging them forms new sentences that change the game. — [Red Bull interview](https://www.redbull.com/ng-en/baba-is-you-interview); [Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/baba-is-you-lets-you-change-the-rules-of-the-game) **[primary/secondary]**
- Teikari's design interview covers balancing freedom against constraint, the pacing and sequencing of puzzles, and what happens when a "cool" design annoys players. — [Nice Games Club ep. 373, "Puzzle Systems (with Arvi Teikari)"](https://nicegames.club/episode/373) **[primary, audio]**
- Outer Wilds is "all knowledge-gated. So if you know everything, you can just boop-da-boop-da-boop" (Beachum). Each major mystery needs roughly three clues or rules to be learned. — [Medium interview with Beachum (cordialkobold)](https://medium.com/@cordialkobold/interview-with-alex-beachum-creative-director-of-outer-wilds-a01bb9631e20); [Medium interview (claire.boeck)](https://medium.com/@claire.boeck.172/interview-with-alex-beachum-ba7e21ee5d17) **[primary words in fan-run interviews; wording may be paraphrased]**
- Beachum's thesis defines "curiosity-driven exploration" as exploration not driven by material rewards (weapons, currency). It says "curiosity, courage, and the desire to understand the universe are emotional and value driven motivations that cannot be easily explained on a spreadsheet". — quoted in [The Examined Game](https://www.theexaminedgame.com/were-all-going-to-die-creating-outer-wilds-alex-beachum-creative-director/) **[primary text, quoted secondhand]**
- A GDC Independent Games Summit talk describes the aim: motivate exploration "without assigned missions", through a "diegetic and player-determined approach to game progression". — [GDC Vault, "Sparking Curiosity"](https://gdcvault.com/play/1027008/Independent-Games-Summit-Sparking-Curiosity) **[primary abstract]**
- "Train, test, twist": no source tracing this phrase to Valve or any named designer was found. Search returned only an unrelated generic AI "skill" page. — [search result](https://skillselion.com/skills/omer-metin/skills-for-antigravity/puzzle-design) **[unreliable]**

### Inferences
- For CS challenges, "one idea per chamber" means each challenge should isolate one concept (for example loops, then off-by-one, then loops plus off-by-one), and Predict/Fix challenges can act as "teaching panels" that open nothing but set up a later Code challenge.
- The laser-versus-energy-ball change is a legibility lesson: prefer mechanics whose effect is always visible. In code challenges, that means visible test output, step traces and diffs rather than an opaque pass/fail.
- Outer Wilds suggests a Floor where knowledge is the key. For example, a password or an algorithm learned on an earlier Floor lets a returning Climber skip ahead, which rewards understanding over grinding.

### Gaps
- Could not read the per-chamber Portal commentary nodes on flinging or momentum directly.
- No primary statement found from Blow describing a step-by-step method for sequencing puzzle "lessons".

## 3. How do they make understanding feel like the player's own ("aha"), and avoid frustration?

### Takeaway
Blow explicitly rejects "trick" aha moments. He wants each epiphany to reveal a true relationship the player actually understood. Valve keeps unplanned solutions when they make testers feel smart and fixes anything testers fail to notice. Teikari prunes alternative solutions that teach nothing. Celeste hides generous input forgiveness so that failure feels fair.

### Cited Findings
- Blow is irritated by puzzles built around the "aha" of getting stuck and then feeling clever. He wants the player to understand something real: "I'm not tricking you into feeling smart… you are getting to exercise your natural intelligence." — [PlayStation LifeStyle](https://www.playstationlifestyle.net/2016/01/04/jonathan-blow-the-witness-puzzles-im-not-tricking-feeling-smart) **[primary]**
- Blow says The Witness is designed to create many opportunities for epiphany, small and large. — [Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/q-a-jonathan-blow-on-i-the-witness-i-and-the-state-of-indie-games); [Game Developer, "The Witness: Modeling epiphany"](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/-i-the-witness-i-modeling-epiphany) **[primary]**. In 2018 he described the goal as simulating epiphany, where what you grasp feels important rather than trivial. — [Gamereactor, "Shared epiphany"](https://www.gamereactor.eu/shared-epiphany-jonathan-blow-reflects-on-the-witness) **[primary]**
- Blow "treats the player as someone who's intelligent". — [Game Developer feature, 2011](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/feature-jonathan-blow-on-designing-i-the-witness-i-) **[primary]**
- Counterpoint: Edge noted that reaching the epiphany takes "an investment of time and mental acrobatics" that many players won't commit to. A review found the game bogs down near the end. — [Edge via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20160210/281522225124768); [GameCloud review](https://gamecloud.net.au/review/the-witness) **[secondary]**
- Portal 2: if a tester finds an unplanned solution that is interesting and makes them feel smart, it may stay as an alternate solution. — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2010/03/17/thinking-with-portals-making-a-test-chamber.aspx) **[primary]**. Portal 1: shortcuts that bypass a puzzle are usually reworked, but are left in when the skip takes more skill than the intended route. — [Portal developer commentary](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_developer_commentary) **[primary]**
- Baba Is You: "a very large part of the testing process" was eliminating alternative solutions that weren't interesting enough. Some unintended solutions stayed because they taught the same lesson in a different structure. — [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-baba-is-you/); [Into Indie Games interview](https://intoindiegames.com/features/2019-best-original-concept-winner-interview-arvi-teikari-baba-is-you/) **[primary]**
- Celeste "forgiveness" mechanics, in Thorson's own write-up: coyote time (a jump still works briefly after leaving a ledge), jump buffering (a jump pressed just before landing fires on the landing frame), halved gravity at the jump peak, corner correction for jumps and dashes, and wall-jump windows. These were first a Twitter thread (March 2020). Thorson notes the team didn't invent these techniques. — [Maddy Thorson, "Celeste & Forgiveness"](https://maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/) **[primary]**; coverage: [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/celeste-dev-explains-how-they-made-their-game-feel/1100-6474775/), [BlizzardWatch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2020/03/13/celeste-developer-game-feel/) **[secondary]**
- Celeste's GDC 2017 talk (as reported) on difficulty: keep it challenging but leave room for creativity. If the player can win no matter how they play, the challenge loses its appeal. — [VidaExtra](https://vidaextra.com/aventura-plataformas/creadores-celeste-pusieron-pinchos-mortales-a-mala-leche-niveles-para-enganarte-forma-brillante-posible) **[secondary]**. The team aimed for "challenging, not frustrating" (PAX West 2017 interview). — [Big Shiny Robot](https://bigshinyrobot.com/video-games/interview-matt-makes-games/) **[primary; page summary only]**

### Inferences
- For code challenges, "forgiveness" maps to hidden grace in grading: normalizing whitespace and trailing newlines, accepting equivalent outputs, ignoring trivial formatting. The comparator should forgive noise, never the concept. Failure must always be on the idea the challenge tests, never on a technicality.
- Prune alternate solutions the way Teikari did. If a Fix challenge can be "solved" by deleting the failing test or hardcoding output, either block it or make sure it still teaches the lesson.
- Blow's line favors challenges where the aha is a true CS insight (for example, why floating point 0.1+0.2 differs), not a gotcha in phrasing.

### Gaps
- No primary measure (such as quit rates) found for any of these games showing how the frustration-avoidance techniques changed player behavior.

## 4. Hints, accessibility (Celeste Assist Mode) and very different skill levels

### Takeaway
Celeste is the clearest primary model: hard by intent, with player-controlled Assist Mode toggles framed as a choice rather than an "easy mode", plus optional collectibles and B-sides for experts. Outer Wilds instead offers an in-world knowledge log (the Ship Log's Rumor Mode). The Witness and Baba Is You largely rely on non-linear structure (many puzzles open at once) rather than explicit hints. No primary source on that was found here.

### Cited Findings
- Thorson: the difficulty is essential to the experience, but people come in with many different skill levels. Strawberries, B-sides and Assist Mode "are all there to help players find the challenge level that's right for them". — [VICE, "Why the Very Hard 'Celeste' is Perfectly Fine With You Breaking Its Rules"](https://www.vice.com/en/article/celeste-difficulty-assist-mode/) **[primary quote]**
- Assist Mode lets players change game speed, stamina, number of air dashes, and even invincibility. The game recommends a first playthrough without it. — [VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/celeste-difficulty-assist-mode/); [Wikipedia, Celeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celeste_(video_game)) **[secondary]**
- A later update changed the Assist Mode intro text from calling the difficulty "essential" to "intended", softening the gatekeeping tone. — [VICE, "The Small But Important Change 'Celeste' Made to Its Celebrated Assist Mode"](https://www.vice.com/en/article/celeste-assist-mode-change-and-accessibility/) **[secondary report of a primary text change]**
- Accessibility-press reception of Assist Mode. — [New Normative](https://newnormative.com/2018/01/25/celestes-assist-mode-brings-welcome-accessibility-options) **[secondary]**
- Outer Wilds Rumor Mode: the Ship Log charts how gathered clues connect and where they may lead, and can mark map locations. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/753640/discussions/0/3455968050315996395/) **[community, low authority]**; [Tom's Guide review](https://www.tomsguide.com/reviews/outer-wilds) **[secondary]**
- The Witness has no overt instructions. Players are left to work things out themselves. — [TechSpot review](https://www.techspot.com/review/1126-the-witness/) **[secondary]**

### Inferences
- A mine-codecraft "Assist" panel could hold separate, player-chosen toggles, not one difficulty slider: more time, extra attempts before the failure cost applies, reveal one failing test case, show a worked example. Present them as a choice without shame, as Celeste does, and keep the strongest optional prestige (the analogue of B-sides or strawberries) for unassisted clears.
- A per-Climber "logbook" like Outer Wilds' Rumor Mode, linking clues found across Floors, could be the hint system for knowledge-gated Floors, so hints are the player's own collected knowledge rather than handed-down answers.

### Gaps
- No primary Mobius statement on Rumor Mode's design history. No primary statements on hint systems from Blow or Teikari (Baba Is You has no built-in hint system as far as found; unverified).
- When and why Assist Mode was added to Celeste during development was not found in a primary source.

## 5. Short retry loops and immediate feedback that make failure informative

### Takeaway
Celeste is the model of fast feedback. Its game-feel tricks make each death feel fair, and the developers built a huge number of short screens so each failure is local. Portal 2's switch to always-visible lasers is the same principle on the puzzle side: the player should always be able to see why something happened. Outer Wilds turns death into a loop that keeps knowledge, so failure itself becomes progress.

### Cited Findings
- Celeste is built from more than 300 short platforming screens (reported from GDC 2017). — [VidaExtra](https://vidaextra.com/aventura-plataformas/creadores-celeste-pusieron-pinchos-mortales-a-mala-leche-niveles-para-enganarte-forma-brillante-posible) **[secondary]**
- Forgiveness mechanics remove "unfair" deaths caused by input timing, so remaining deaths read as the player's own error. — [Thorson, "Celeste & Forgiveness"](https://maddymakesgames.com/articles/celeste_and_forgiveness/) **[primary]**
- Portal 2 chose lasers because they "always let the player know how they will affect the environment" (Faliszek, GDC 2012). — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-2012-i-portal-2-i-making-a-sequel-to-a-perfect-game) **[primary report]**
- Outer Wilds: knowledge carries across deaths, and the player keeps going "to answer their questions" (Beachum). — [Medium interview](https://medium.com/@claire.boeck.172/interview-with-alex-beachum-ba7e21ee5d17) **[primary in fan interview]**. On its loop and mortality themes: [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/gaming/outer-wilds-is-a-game-that-asks-what-if-you-only-had-20-minutes-to-live-1288956) **[secondary]**

### Inferences
- Map this to a cost for failure. Keep the cost (losing a Floor attempt, a boss reset) at the Floor or boss level, but make the inner loop of submit, see the failing case, edit and resubmit fast and free, like Celeste's instant respawn inside a screen. Feedback should name what failed (the input, expected output, actual output), like Portal's visible lasers.
- An Outer Wilds-style rule: what a Climber learned survives failure. Failing a boss should leave behind a clue (which test failed, which concept), so the next attempt starts smarter.

### Gaps
- No primary source found quantifying Celeste's respawn time, or describing death counts as a design decision. Search found nothing reliable.
